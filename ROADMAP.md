## Release direction

**1.1 focuses on faster, more accurate food logging and correction, plus Watch preset buttons.** Its core is weighed and volume-based food portions, editing logged food, the missing meal-composition workflows, restaurant and brand nutrition lookup for meal photos, missing-nutrition indicators, and Watch presets. C5 adds the photo-plus-brand workflow, such as a Chipotle meal, with published nutrition and source links. C6 adds measured liquid portions; C7 distinguishes known nutrition from missing information and partial totals. Standalone food search by name remains in the backlog under its own gate.

**Standing rule:** Logalyst does not build anything Apple Health already has or Apple has announced. Check each overlap question against the current iOS release or beta (the iOS 27.2 beta as of October 3, 2026) before building the feature, and recheck before release. Compare the complete interaction, not just whether Health stores the same metric: an equivalent Apple workflow removes the feature, and an unresolved question keeps it held, or flagged as a validation item, until it is checked. A feature can also be deferred for product focus without claiming Apple offers an exact equivalent.

Sizes below are relative planning estimates, not delivery dates: Small is a contained change; Medium spans several flows or needs recovery/synchronization work; Large introduces substantial data or service dependencies. The core takes precedence: optional scope is added only under the rule in "Optional additions to 1.1" and must not delay the core.

Four sizes need explanation. Editing logged food is Medium rather than Small, because a Health entry cannot be changed in place and the save-then-delete replacement needs recovery work. Notes on entries is Small–Medium rather than Small, because its storage and synchronization are undefined. C4 is Medium although its two meal-screen gaps are small in themselves, because it covers every path into the meal screen and C2's weighed portions. Goal adherence is unsized until its gate is settled.

C5 is Large because it needs nutrition and search providers, reliable matching and attribution, and a new network/privacy design. Its inclusion in 1.1 is decided and it's implemented, reading brands' own websites (so it has no provider or operating cost); validation with real photos and on a device remains.

### C1. Watch preset buttons

- Show each metric's presets on the Watch's numeric (Digital Crown) entry screen. The Watch already holds them: lists edited on iPhone arrive through the existing sync, and unedited units use the built-in lists compiled into both apps. Until the user saves their own on iPhone, only Water, Caffeine, Alcoholic Drinks, and Inhaler Use have presets. A unit can hold up to six, so the layout must fit six alongside the amount and Save.
- Tapping a preset fills the amount; retain Digital Crown adjustment and the existing Save action. Check on a device how an amount off the Crown's step (a custom 330 mL, for example) behaves when the Crown is turned afterwards.
- Show the list for the unit the Watch enters in. The Watch has no unit picker and does not receive the iPhone's Options → Units choice, so it uses the Health-preference or regional unit, and presets saved on iPhone under a different unit will not appear on the Watch. Decide before starting whether to accept and document this or to add the iPhone's unit choices to the iPhone-to-Watch sync; recheck the Small estimate if the sync is extended.
- Keep the stored order (always smallest first; there is no custom ordering), use the built-in list for a unit the user has not edited, and show no buttons where the user removed every preset. Preset management stays on iPhone.
- Decided: the Watch shows the presets of the unit it enters in, and the iPhone's unit choice is not synced (Open decisions 2). The README and support page say so.

**Ready when:** the Watch shows the presets for the unit it enters in, smallest first; a unit the user has not edited shows the built-in list, and a unit whose presets were all removed shows none; an iPhone preset change reaches the Watch by the next time the Watch app opens; presets never cross units; selecting a preset fills the amount without saving; and the existing in-flight Save guard (already on iPhone and Watch in build 25) still prevents a repeated Save tap from logging twice. C1 has no dependency on the food work. Whether it is released ahead of the rest of 1.1 is an open decision; until that is decided, it ships with 1.1 under the release gate.

### C2. Log food and recipe ingredients by weight

- Add an optional numeric serving weight in grams to saved foods and food portions, while retaining the human-readable serving description. Serving size is free text in both today, so food can be logged only in servings.
- Accept grams or ounces when a reliable serving weight exists; calculate nutrition from the equivalent number of servings. Keep serving-based entry available when weight is unknown.
- Carry weight information through the saved-food editor, barcode imports, label scanning, recipe ingredient snapshots, recent-food and recent-meal re-logging (including the Log Again actions in Add Food, History, and Nutrition), logged-food metadata, and the portion summaries shown in Recents, the meal screen, and the recipe editor, so a weighed entry reads as "35 g" rather than "0.35 × 100 g". Meal-photo estimates carry no weight and stay servings-only unless an item matches a saved food, which then brings its weight along, or C5 retrieves a reliable published serving weight. A photo alone must not create a verified serving weight. Widgets, Siri/Shortcuts, and the Watch app do not read the serving model today and need no change.
- Support weighed recipe ingredients. Do not infer a finished recipe's weight from ingredient weights. The recipe model has no weight today (a recipe is logged as a fraction such as "1/4 recipe"), so finished recipes stay logged by the serving in 1.1. Logging a finished recipe by weight would need its own per-serving weight on the recipe and is not part of C2 unless added here explicitly.
- Extend the three places serving data is stored today, without losing foods or recipes:
  - **Saved foods** are SwiftData models mirrored to the user's private iCloud (CloudKit) database, with no versioned schema or migration plan. The weight must be an optional or defaulted attribute, as iCloud syncing requires of every field, and marked for iCloud encryption like the existing fields, so the privacy policy's statement that every field is end-to-end encrypted stays true.
  - **Recipe ingredients** are stored inside each recipe as a JSON-encoded list of food portions that loads as an empty list if decoding fails. Make the weight an optional key, or decode it with a fallback; a required key would make every existing recipe load with no ingredients and no error.
  - **Logged entries** keep servings, serving text, brand, and meal as metadata on the Health food entry. Add a weight metadata key for new entries; entries already in Health cannot be rewritten.
- Older records and logged entries without weight metadata must remain readable and usable by servings.
- Decide how foods saved before 1.1 get a weight. Their serving is text only, including the "100 g" the barcode lookup writes when a product lists no serving, so the 35 g case will not work for an already-saved food until it has one. Options: enter it in the food editor, or offer a weight parsed from an unambiguous gram amount in the existing text (the label scanner already parses text like "1 bar (30 g)"). Never back-fill a weight the text does not state. Recents re-log the portion read back from Health rather than the saved food, so also decide whether an entry logged before 1.1 picks up the weight of a saved food with the same name and brand or stays servings-only.
- Both import paths already reduce nutrients to one serving; what C2 adds is the numeric weight. The barcode lookup prefers Open Food Facts' per-serving values and otherwise uses its per-100 values with the serving text "100 g". The label scanner scales a per-100 g or per-100 mL column to the printed serving when the units match, and otherwise calls the serving "100 g" or "100 mL". One existing behavior must change: the barcode fallback writes "100 g" even though Open Food Facts defines its per-100 values as per 100 g or 100 mL, so a drink with no listed serving must not gain a 100 g weight. The lookup reads only the serving text today, so a per-serving product's weight has to come from that text or from additional database fields.
- Do not treat milliliters or fluid ounces as mass without a known conversion. C6 separately adds volume entry for foods with an explicit serving volume; C2 remains the weight implementation.

**Ready when:** a 35 g portion of a food defined per 100 g logs 0.35 times each nutrient; the same portion entered in ounces logs the same nutrients to displayed precision; foods created by barcode import and by label scanning can be logged by weight when the source states a gram serving weight; a serving stated only in milliliters or fluid ounces never gains an inferred weight (C6 governs its volume entry); recipes created before the upgrade show unchanged per-serving nutrition, and a recipe with weighed ingredients totals its ingredients' scaled nutrients; and existing foods and recipes survive the upgrade from build 25, an iCloud round trip, and local-only use, with entries lacking weight metadata still loggable by servings. Ambiguous or missing weights must not silently produce guessed nutrition.

### C3. Edit logged food

- Open a food logged by Logalyst from History or Nutrition and change servings, meal, or date and time. Support weight entry when the entry carries C2's weight metadata, and volume entry when it carries C6's volume metadata. Preserve C7's known-zero and missing-nutrition state through replacement and recovery. History lists only the 200 most recent entries of any kind and Nutrition lists only today's foods, so older foods stay out of reach until the food diary (a later candidate) adds past-day navigation.
- Recalculate nutrients from the logged entry itself, which is its snapshot: the entry's nutrient samples hold the totals and its metadata holds the servings, serving text, brand, and meal, so per-serving nutrition is the totals divided by the servings (entries from before servings were recorded count as one serving). Nothing links a logged entry to a saved food except a matching name and brand, so editing or deleting the saved-library counterpart does not change the entry's basis. No new snapshot storage is needed for serving-based edits; weighed edits need the weight metadata C2 adds.
- Replace the Health food entry and its nutrient samples, then remove the original entry and its samples. Treat this as a multi-step operation that can partially fail.
- Editing already works this way for every non-food entry: the entry screen saves the corrected entry, then deletes the original, and if the delete fails it tells the user both are in History and to swipe away the one they do not want. It has no retry and keeps nothing across a relaunch. Matching that flow for foods is the smaller part of C3; the recovery below goes beyond what ships today and is why C3 is Medium. Decide whether the stronger recovery also replaces the non-food behavior or applies to foods only, so the two edit paths do not diverge by accident.
- Preserve the original if saving the replacement fails. If replacement succeeds but cleanup fails, show the incomplete state and provide a recovery path that retries cleanup without saving another replacement.
- Retain enough operation state to reconcile an interrupted edit after relaunch. Today's Health helpers do not provide this: the food save returns nothing and builds its Health entry privately, delete needs an entry already loaded from Health, entries can be re-read only through the recent-entries query (there is no lookup by identifier), and no edit state is stored anywhere. C3 includes extending them so the pending original/replacement pair is recorded before the save and found again after a relaunch.
- Refresh affected days, recents, totals, and widgets after completion or recovery. The Health store's existing save and delete paths already trigger this (they reload Nutrition, History, and Recents, reload widget timelines, and reschedule log reminders), so route edits and recovery through them rather than adding a separate refresh mechanism.

**Ready when:** successful edits leave one corrected food entry and the expected nutrient totals; an injected save failure leaves the original entry unchanged; an injected delete failure shows the incomplete state, and a retry removes only the original without saving another replacement; an edit interrupted by app termination is reconciled after relaunch; affected days, recents, totals, and widgets refresh after completion or recovery; and when the entry carries C2 weight metadata, changing the weight recalculates nutrients, while entries without it remain editable by servings. Do not claim duplicate entries are impossible during a partial failure. Editing a logged food must not modify the saved food or recipe definition, including its last-logged date: today's logging screens set that date on every save, which would move an edited food to the top of My Foods.

### C4. Compose meals and correct meal-photo items

Extend the existing meal screen with the two missing workflows:

1. Start a meal by selecting multiple saved foods, without first creating a recipe.
2. Add a missing food or replace an incorrectly identified item in the meal review screen, including results from a meal photo.

Reuse the existing portion adjustment, item exclusion, combined nutrition preview, meal/time controls, and Save as Recipe flow, plus the recipe editor's Add Ingredient picker (search saved foods or create one), which today is private to the recipe editor and offers saved foods only. These are existing capabilities, not new features to rebuild. Integrate C2's weighed portions, C6's volume portions and C7's partial-nutrition indicators into the same review flow.

Apple overlap check: iOS 27's Siri mode in Camera gives nutritional information about a plate of food on Apple Intelligence iPhones. Apple's material does not describe logging that result to Health, and beta-period press coverage (MacRumors, iGeeksBlog) reports that it gives no exact calorie counts and records nothing in Health. C4 stays distinct because it composes, corrects, and logs itemized portions. Recheck this on a device before release positioning.

**Ready when:** every path into the meal screen (a recent meal, a meal photo, and the new manual start) supports adding, replacing, adjusting, and excluding foods; portions can be entered by weight or volume when the corresponding serving basis is known, and by servings otherwise; preview totals and their coverage match the logged result; and saving as a recipe preserves the chosen ingredients, amounts and missing-nutrition state. Manual meal composition must work where meal-photo analysis is unavailable: the app runs on iOS 18 and later, while Photo of Meal needs iOS 27 with Apple Intelligence available, and its button is hidden otherwise. Validate the new manual start on an iOS 18–26 device or with Apple Intelligence off.

### C5. Look up restaurant and brand nutrition for meal photos

**Committed to 1.1 by the owner on October 3, 2026, and implemented the same day (see As built below).** Take or choose a photo, supply a restaurant or brand such as "Chipotle," and use published nutrition for matching foods before reviewing and logging the meal.

- **Photo and context:** add optional Restaurant or brand and Meal details fields, with details such as "double chicken, white rice, black beans, no sour cream." Let the user enter or change them after choosing the photo and before submitting analysis. Explain that the branded lookup searches online, and send no requests while the user types. Leaving the brand blank retains the existing on-device estimate flow.
- **AI and lookup:** keep photo analysis on the iPhone. Give the model an app-provided lookup tool to request nutrition for the restaurant/brand and identified items. Prefer structured nutrition records where available, with web search of official restaurant or manufacturer pages as the fallback. Match the brand, menu item or ingredient, market, and serving basis; ask the user to resolve ambiguous matches. Chipotle is the first acceptance case, and another restaurant or brand must exercise the same lookup path before release.
- **Ingredients and portions:** combine visible foods with the user's details, and ask for confirmation of hidden ingredients or uncertain additions, such as rice beneath toppings or double protein. Use published per-serving values for confirmed matches and calculate scaled nutrients in app code through C2's portion model. Keep estimated quantities visibly distinct from published nutrition values. Missing nutrients stay unknown, and a gram serving weight is used only when the source states one. A similarly named saved food must not silently replace a confirmed branded match.
- **Sources and review:** retain the source title, URL, retrieval date, market, and serving basis with each retrieved result. Show source links and identify photo estimates or unmatched items in C4's existing review screen. Support adding, replacing, adjusting and excluding items, then logging or saving as a recipe. Preserve the reviewed nutrient snapshot and its source/estimate context when saving and re-logging; later website changes must not rewrite earlier entries. Retrieved page content supplies evidence only and must not override the app's instructions or trigger logging.
- **Failure handling:** bound lookup requests and latency, cache results with their source and freshness, and handle cancellation, no match, partial results, unavailable pages, offline use and rate limits. Offer retry, manual correction or clearly labeled on-device estimates when lookup fails. Never claim that estimated nutrition was verified online, and never log without the existing review and Log action.
- **Privacy:** send only the restaurant/brand, relevant food terms, and a selected market when needed for lookup. Keep photos, photo metadata, full free-text notes, saved-food libraries and Health data on the device. Identify every provider and any Logalyst-operated service, including query handling and retention. Update `PRIVACY.md`, its website output, release notes and applicable privacy declarations before shipping; the current statements that only barcode lookups send food information online will no longer describe C5.

**Implementation decisions to resolve:** choose nutrition and web-search providers after checking coverage (including Chipotle), supported access, freshness, attribution/licensing, limits, latency and cost; decide whether requests can run directly from the app or need a service to protect credentials (no shared secret embedded in the app); define cache expiry and query retention; and specify backward-compatible storage for source/estimate context across foods, recipes and logged meals. Resolve these for C5 without pulling standalone food search by name into 1.1. Apple's tool calling supplies the integration mechanism, not a nutrition database or search service. Chipotle's public calculator establishes an official source to investigate, not an approved API integration.

**Ready when:** a real Chipotle meal photo with the brand supplied produces editable ingredient matches grounded in retrieved nutrition; another restaurant or brand works through the same provider path; a confirmed double portion scales each published nutrient exactly twice; hidden ingredients and ambiguous matches can be corrected before logging; each published result links to its source and clearly separates its serving basis from estimated quantity; sources with missing nutrients or incompatible units do not produce fabricated values; saved and re-logged meals retain their reviewed values and source/estimate context; offline, no-match, partial-result, cancellation and rate-limit cases offer a usable fallback; a photo without a brand still works on-device; request inspection confirms only the documented query data leaves the phone; and provider access, privacy updates and device/Apple Health checks are complete. Record provider, source dates, test meals, device and OS build with the validation evidence. If C5 cannot meet these conditions, changing its 1.1 scope or release timing is an owner decision.

**As built (October 3, 2026).** Decisions, and why:

- **Brands' own websites, no provider.** A first version used fatsecret through a Logalyst-operated API, since of the sources checked on October 3 (Open Food Facts, USDA FoodData Central, fatsecret, Nutritionix, Edamam, Spoonacular and MenuStat) only fatsecret listed Chipotle's ingredients. *Owner direction, October 3, 2026: don't rely on fatsecret; have the on-device model find the brand's nutrition online.* So the iPhone finds and reads the brand's own published nutrition: Apple Maps gives the website of a restaurant (cafés, bakeries and food markets too) named like the brand; for a brand Maps doesn't list, the on-device model suggests a domain, used only if the site's name begins with the brand's. A site under another country's domain isn't used. This needs no account, key, server or operating cost, and only the brand's name leaves the iPhone.
- **Reading the site.** A hidden web view opens the site (at www first), then the best nutrition pages its links and its sitemap name, up to four, until one links a nutrition PDF, of which it reads two. Chipotle's calculator links its *Full Nutrition Facts* PDF only from its meal pages, which its sitemap lists. Links for another country (Wendy's United Kingdom guide) are left out. The web view keeps no cookies and blocks images, frames, beacons and pop-ups from other companies, and all requests to a list of advertising, analytics and session-recording services; other companies' scripts and data requests are allowed because sites such as Chipotle's need them to show anything.
- **Tables are read directly; the model reads other text.** The owner asked whether the model could do the reading instead of a parser. Tried on Chipotle's table with the on-device model, it copied the values of each food it found exactly, but given all the foods at once it skipped three of six that were there, and asked one food at a time it found all of them but put two rows' values in the wrong columns (white rice's "calories from fat" as its fat) and matched a lime wedge to watermelon limeade. Those numbers are printed on the food's line, so nothing could catch them before they were logged. So nutrition tables are read by code: the header names the columns (labels one per line or wrapped, with units; a "Serving Size (g)" column is a stated weight), each row needs exactly one value per column, and a row with a number left over is dropped. Other text (a menu page, a list of items with their nutrients) goes to the on-device model, and a value is kept only when it's printed beside its nutrient's name on the food's line and the calories add up to within 35% of what the fat, carbohydrates and protein give. Retrieved text never reaches the model that reads the photo, and it can't trigger logging.
- **Matching in app code, not model tool calling.** The photo model returns each item with a short search term, its multiple of the brand's standard portion, whether it was seen, described or may be hidden, and an estimate. Items are matched to the brand's foods by their words (the last word, usually what the food is, counts double, so "rice" fits Cilantro-Lime White Rice and "lime" doesn't; common short names like "guac" and "veggies" are spelled out), and the user is asked whenever foods fit equally. Searching for a food in the picker searches what was read, on the iPhone.
- **Markets.** No market is sent anywhere. Sites and links for another country are avoided; a source's market is recorded only if known.
- **Caching.** What's read from a brand's site is reused for 24 hours in memory, and matched results for 24 hours in the app's Caches folder (not backed up or synced), each with the date it was read.
- **Failures.** A lookup gives up after 75 seconds (finding the site, opening a few pages, reading a PDF and, for other text, the model); Stop cancels. Offline, timeout, busy and unreadable sites give the reason, Try Again, and on-device estimates marked as such; if nothing is found, the brand's nutrition pages (or its site) are linked.
- **Storage.** C5 added optional `source` (title, URL, read date, market, serving basis, website) and `isEstimate` fields to `FoodPortion`. Recipe ingredients store those as optional JSON keys, read leniently and left out when unset. Build 25 drops them when it rewrites a recipe. Health food entries store them as `HealthLogger…` metadata, so logging again and editing keep them. C5 itself added no saved-food fields; C6 subsequently adds an encrypted serving-volume field and C7 adds ingredient coverage to snapshots.
- **Review.** A clear match counts at the published values times the portions; one that fits several waits at zero with Choose (or Use the Estimate); a possibly hidden food waits with Include; a food the brand doesn't list is an estimate that says so. Rows say "Published per 4 oz" or "Estimate", and Sources links each document with its website and the date it was read. Replacing or adding a food offers the brand's foods, filtered as you type.
- **Photo of Meal now waits** for Estimate Nutrition or Look Up Nutrition after the photo is chosen, so the brand and details can be entered first. Details also inform the on-device estimate and are never sent.

### C6. Food portions by volume

**Committed to the 1.1 core and implemented at the owner's request on October 3, 2026; simulator validation complete, with device and iCloud checks remaining.** Log a measured liquid food portion, such as 180 mL of milk whose nutrition is stated per 100 mL, using the same review and correction flows as weighed foods.

- Store an optional explicit serving volume in milliliters alongside serving text and any separately known weight. Allow mL and clearly identified fluid-ounce units; distinguish U.S. from Imperial fluid ounces and both from weight ounces. Ambiguous source units require confirmation rather than an assumed conversion.
- Scale each known nutrient by entered volume divided by serving volume. Keep servings entry available when volume is absent or ambiguous. Never assume 1 mL equals 1 g, use a per-100-g nutrition column as per-100-mL nutrition, or infer volume from a photo.
- Preserve the physical amount when switching volume units. When replacing a meal item, carry its volume only if the replacement has a compatible volume basis; otherwise ask for a serving amount instead of treating the mL number as a serving count.
- Support volume entry in the food editor, food logging and editing, meal composition and correction, and recipe ingredients. Carry it through barcode and label imports when the source gives a compatible basis, and through C5's published portions when explicitly stated. Do not infer a finished recipe's volume from its ingredients; finished recipes remain serving-based.
- Preserve serving volume, selected portion and unit in saved foods, recipe ingredient snapshots and Health food-entry metadata, so editing and re-logging retain the original basis and display an amount such as "180 mL". Existing records without volume remain usable. Offer unambiguous volume parsed from older serving text for review rather than silently changing stored nutrition.
- Use backward-compatible optional storage, encrypted CloudKit fields where applicable, and the existing edit recovery. Validate upgrades, local-only use, iCloud round trips and mixed-version edits; define what happens when an older app drops new volume metadata. No new food-logging surface on Watch or new Shortcuts parameters are required.

**Ready when:** 180 mL of a food defined per 100 mL logs 1.8 times each known nutrient; the same amount in a supported fluid-ounce unit gives equivalent nutrition; U.S./Imperial fluid ounces and mass ounces cannot be confused; per-volume barcode, label and published-source fixtures retain their serving basis; missing or ambiguous volumes fall back to servings without inventing a conversion; volume portions survive logging, editing, re-logging, recipes and iCloud; old records still load; and the Apple Health and device checks pass.

**As built (October 3, 2026).** Foods carry an optional `millilitersPerServing`, encrypted in CloudKit; ingredient snapshots and Health food metadata preserve that basis and the selected volume unit. Entry supports mL, U.S. fluid ounces (29.5735295625 mL) and Imperial fluid ounces (28.4130625 mL), keeping the physical amount when units change. Food editing, logging, logged-food edits, meal correction and recipe ingredients share this model; finished recipes remain serving-based. Barcode, label and published-source imports use only compatible, explicit volume information. Bare fluid ounces or conflicting source amounts do not gain a guessed conversion. Old serving text is offered for review without automatically changing the stored nutrition basis. A replacement lacking a compatible measured basis requires a serving amount.

The checked-in CloudKit schema declares `CD_Food.CD_millilitersPerServing` as `ENCRYPTED DOUBLE`; development import and production deployment must be verified before distribution. This is a new field beyond those deployed for build 26. An older app can drop volume metadata when rewriting a record; after such an edit, review the serving basis again. The new app cannot infer lost volume from weight or nutrition. Unit/HealthKit, targeted volume UI and default/largest-text interaction and relaunch tests passed (see Evidence); visual-audit findings remain documented. No physical-device or iCloud round trip is established by this implementation.

### C7. Missing-nutrition indicators

**Committed to the 1.1 core and implemented at the owner's request on October 3, 2026; simulator validation complete, with device and iCloud checks remaining.** Make absent nutrition and partial totals visible, such as "Sodium unavailable for 2 ingredients," without presenting unknown values as measured zero.

- Preserve an explicitly entered or published zero separately from an absent nutrient through food editing, imports, source lookup, portion scaling, recipes and Health metadata. Missing fields in older records remain unknown; they cannot retrospectively prove a zero. Unknown nutrients must not generate fabricated zero-valued Health samples.
- Show missing nutrients in food review and editing. In meal and recipe summaries, total the available values and identify partial coverage per nutrient, counting only included ingredients with positive portions. If every included ingredient lacks a nutrient, show it as unavailable rather than as a numeric zero. Retain the existing Estimate and Published labels; availability does not establish accuracy.
- Carry completeness information through recipe aggregation, saving, logging, editing and re-logging. A recipe's summed sodium must not become "complete" just because the missing ingredient detail was collapsed into one food entry. Preserve the nutrition snapshot instead of recalculating old logs from today's food library.
- In Nutrition, distinguish a numeric zero from "No recorded data available" and label totals affected by known missing nutrition as partial. Keep totals based on intake recorded in Health, including other apps. Where legacy or other-app records lack coverage metadata, do not invent a missing-item count or imply the whole day's intake is complete. An empty Health query does not establish that nothing was consumed or reveal whether read permission was denied.
- Ensure affected goal messages, widgets and spoken summaries do not declare a limit met from missing data or a partial total; concise unknown/partial wording may replace a detailed ingredient count on compact surfaces. The feature describes data coverage, not dietary advice, a nutrition score or the separate goal-adherence backlog feature.

**Ready when:** an explicit zero stays distinguishable from an omitted value after every storage round trip; a meal with one known sodium value and two included ingredients without sodium shows the known subtotal and two missing ingredients; an all-unknown nutrient shows unavailable; omitted or zero-portion ingredients do not inflate the count; recipe aggregation and later edits retain partial coverage; empty, unreadable, legacy and other-app data receive honest states; compact and spoken summaries do not turn missing information into success; and the Apple Health, device and accessibility checks pass.

**As built (October 3, 2026).** A present nutrient key with value zero is known; an absent key is unknown. Food editing leaves unavailable fields blank and accepts a food whose stated nutrients are all zero. Imports retain published zeros and omit unknown values; photo estimates use a separate unknown sentinel rather than filling missing estimates with zero. Per-nutrient coverage records known and missing ingredient counts, excluding nonpositive portions. Recipe snapshots and Health metadata preserve coverage through aggregation, editing and re-logging. Meal and recipe summaries display known subtotals as partial when ingredients are missing, and all-unknown nutrients as unavailable. Daily and compact views distinguish no recorded data from zero and qualify incomplete totals rather than declaring goal or limit success.

HealthKit requires a food correlation to contain at least one nutrient sample. Direct logging therefore requires at least one known nutrient per food, including a stated zero; it never creates a zero placeholder for an entirely unknown food. A meal with such an ingredient can be saved as a recipe alongside known nutrition, preserving the unknown ingredient's coverage in the aggregate. The review screen explains this and blocks direct logging until the food is corrected or left out.

Older or other-app records do not establish complete coverage, and an empty Health query cannot distinguish no recorded data from denied read access. Older app versions can drop coverage metadata and may discard explicit zero values when they rewrite data. Those losses cannot be reconstructed from totals; review affected nutrition after mixed-version edits. Unit/HealthKit, the zero/unknown volume UI case and the two-missing-ingredient/exclusion/persisted-recipe UI scenario passed. The default/largest-text interaction and relaunch scenario also passed; visual-audit follow-up, physical-device, mixed-version iCloud and VoiceOver checks remain open.

### Food-search gate

Standalone food search by name is not part of the 1.1 commitment and does not set its release date. C5's restaurant/brand lookup for meal photos is committed to 1.1 and resolves its own provider and privacy dependencies as core work. The questions below govern the broader search screen; investigate them without delaying the core, and schedule that broader feature only after all five are resolved and a separate scope decision is made. Reuse provider and privacy decisions from C5 where applicable.

1. **Coverage and access:** choose packaged-food and generic-food sources. The working proposal is Open Food Facts for packaged foods and a second source such as USDA FoodData Central for generic foods like "banana".
   - Confirm which Open Food Facts endpoint will serve name search before estimating. Its API documentation says API v2 offers structured, filter-based search only, API v3 has no search, and full-text search is offered only through the legacy `/cgi/search.pl` endpoint, with a separate Search-a-licious service (search.openfoodfacts.org) intended to provide it. No limits or terms specific to that service were found. Record the chosen endpoint's stability, limits, and terms, and treat a change of endpoint as a known risk.
   - Decide whether generic-food access uses a proxy, user-provided credentials, or a bundled/downloaded dataset. USDA publishes its generic-food datasets as public-domain (CC0) downloads, so the bundled or downloaded option is viable in principle: in the October 2026 listing, Foundation Foods is about 0.5 MB zipped (6.5 MB as JSON), FNDDS 2021–2023 about 3.7 MB (64 MB), and SR Legacy about 12 MB (205 MB as JSON) and no longer updated. The Branded dataset is 195 MB zipped (3.1 GB) and is not a bundling candidate. Measure the app-size or download cost of a trimmed dataset before choosing. A local generic dataset would need no API key and would send no generic-food search terms off the device.
   - Do not ship an extractable shared publisher API key.
2. **Interaction:** keep saved-library filtering local. Use an explicit Search online submission rather than sending each keystroke. Include caching, pagination, request throttling, rate-limit recovery, and clear offline/error states.
3. **Normalization:** map per-serving and per-100-g nutrients into C2's model, preserve source/portion context, and let the user review the result before saving or logging.
4. **Privacy and operations:** identify which queries and identifiers leave the device, their destinations, and any server retention. Today the only request the app sends to a third-party service is the barcode lookup: the barcode number, an app-name User-Agent and, as with any request, the device's IP address go to Open Food Facts, and only when no saved food matches. Any online search, even one sent directly to Open Food Facts, contradicts two current policy statements: that a barcode number is the only other thing that leaves the device, and that the app never connects to the internet unless a barcode is scanned or typed (apart from the App Store, for the Tip Jar). A second provider adds a new recipient to disclose. A proxy additionally requires revisiting the claim that Logalyst has no servers of its own, and rechecking the app's privacy manifest (which declares no collected data) and the App Store privacy answers. Update `PRIVACY.md` before shipping (the website privacy page is generated from it), change its effective date, and note the change in the release notes, as the policy promises.
5. **Licensing, attribution, and identification:** Open Food Facts publishes its database under the Open Database License (ODbL), its contents under the Database Contents License, and product images under CC BY-SA. Its reuse terms require naming the licence and attributing Open Food Facts with a link, and its data page asks apps for prominent attribution on product and search screens and in an About or Settings section. It also asks integrators to send a User-Agent of the form `AppName/Version (ContactEmail)` and to fill in its API usage form. A publicly used adapted database must be offered under the ODbL, so confirm how share-alike applies before choosing a proxy cache or bundled dataset that combines Open Food Facts records with another source, and keep the sources separable. USDA FoodData Central data is public domain (CC0); USDA asks that FoodData Central be listed as the source. Open Food Facts states its data is indicative and not for medical use, so keep the existing prompt to check imported values against the label.

Provider facts, checked against the providers' published documentation on October 3, 2026. Recheck them when the architecture is chosen, because limits can change.

- **Open Food Facts** ([API introduction](https://openfoodfacts.github.io/openfoodfacts-server/api/)): search is limited to 10 requests per minute per IP address and product (barcode) reads to 15 per minute. The documentation says not to use search for search-as-you-type, which is the reason for the explicit Search online step, and exceeding the limits can lead to an IP ban. When requests come directly from users' devices the limits apply per user, so a shared proxy would put every user behind one limit unless it serves its own copy of the data.
- **USDA FoodData Central** ([API guide](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/api-guide/), [downloadable datasets](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/download-datasets/)): every request needs a data.gov API key. The key holder is responsible for keeping it from becoming public, and keys found online are deactivated. The default limit is 1,000 requests per hour per IP address, and exceeding it blocks the key for an hour. The shared `DEMO_KEY` is limited to 30 requests per hour and 50 per day per IP address, so it is not a shipping option.

The shipped barcode lookup already uses Open Food Facts, whether or not food search ships. *Done October 3, 2026:* it identifies itself as `Logalyst/<version> (support@logalyst.app)` instead of `HealthLogger/1.0 (iOS)`, the food editor's note on an imported food links to Open Food Facts and its Open Database License, and the privacy policy lists the version and contact address among what is sent. It still calls API v2, which Open Food Facts now marks deprecated but still supported (v3 is recommended for new integrations). Moving to v3 is not a C2 completion condition; decide it when the import code is next changed.

## Build sequence and release gate

1. **Validate against Apple Health:** the planned first step was to check before implementation whether Apple offers an equivalent to weighed portions, editing a logged food entry, multi-food meal entry, C5's complete photo-plus-brand lookup and review workflow, C6's volume-based food portions, or C7's missing-nutrition and partial-total indicators. All are now implemented at the owner's request with outstanding device checks, so this remains a release gate. Compare the current iOS release and beta, and record the device, build, and date under Evidence. If Apple covers a workflow, move that item to Held or excluded.
2. **Build the contained improvement first:** implement C1 against existing preset synchronization, once its unit decision is made.
3. **Establish the food foundation:** define and migrate C2's numeric weight data, then connect imports, recipes, logging, and re-logging. In parallel, design and test C3's replacement/cleanup recovery. Before distributing a 1.1 build, add the new fields (`Food.gramsPerServing`, and `Food.uuid` and `Recipe.uuid` for Shortcuts) to the CloudKit development schema and deploy them to production. The Debug `-InitializeCloudKitSchema YES` launch argument exists for this, because syncing alone creates only fields that have held a value, and App Store builds can reach only the production environment. A field cannot be deleted once it is in production, so settle its name and type first.
4. **Complete correction and composition:** deliver C3 and C4 using the shared portion calculations and existing meal UI. C3's servings, meal, and time edits and C4's servings-based composition do not depend on C2 and can be completed first if C2 slips.
5. **Connect restaurant and brand lookup:** resolve C5's providers, access, costs, credentials, source storage and privacy design, then implement photo context, lookup and matching through C2's portion calculations and C4's review screen. Validate Chipotle and another brand, retrieved-source accuracy, portion scaling, request contents, and failure/fallback paths on an Apple Intelligence iPhone. Complete the privacy and release-note changes before shipping.
6. **Validate volume and nutrition coverage:** C6/C7's shared model, imports, review, aggregation, logging, daily and compact presentation are implemented. Verify deployment of the optional encrypted `Food.millilitersPerServing` field to CloudKit Development and Production before distribution. Complete both features' Ready-when checks, including the behavior of older apps dropping volume/coverage or zero values while editing records.
7. **Assess capacity:** add optional 1.1 features in the order above only if they do not delay core validation. Investigate standalone food search by name separately.
8. **Validate the release:** exercise upgrades from build 25, iCloud round trips and local-only use, foods without weight or volume metadata, recipe snapshots, explicit-zero and unknown nutrients, partial totals, permission failures, interrupted edits, meal composition and correction (including on a device where meal-photo analysis is unavailable), C5's sourced results and offline/error fallbacks, and Watch preset synchronization. Confirm Health totals and UI refreshes after logging, editing, deleting, and re-logging. After upgrading from build 25, confirm the saved-food store still opens with iCloud sync on: the app falls back to a device-only store, without telling the user, if the iCloud-backed store cannot be created, and stops at launch if neither opens. Include a second iPhone still on build 25 syncing the same iCloud data: it must keep working, and what happens to weights when a food or recipe is edited there must be observed and decided (build 25 rewrites a recipe's ingredient list without any weight key). Check C5's source metadata, C6's volume metadata and C7's coverage state against older records and mixed-version edits as well.

Release 1.1 when all of the following hold:

1. C1–C7 each meet their Ready-when conditions.
2. The "Validate the release" step passes, including the injected C3 failures and Health permission failures.
3. Each shipped feature has been rechecked against Apple Health on the then-current iOS release and beta, with device and build recorded under Evidence.
4. User-facing documentation describes the shipped behavior: the README, website support page, release notes, and the App Store listing where it describes affected behavior. C5's new network requests and providers are reflected in `PRIVACY.md`, the generated website privacy page and applicable privacy declarations; remove the draft release-note claim that nothing new leaves the device.
5. New and changed screens have been validated with VoiceOver and supported text sizes.

An unfinished optional feature moves to the backlog rather than becoming an implicit release blocker. If a core feature cannot meet its condition, cutting it or delaying 1.1 is an owner decision.
