Support

Need help with Logalyst, found a bug, or have an idea? Email support@logalyst.app and include your iPhone and iOS version (and Apple Watch and watchOS version, if it's about the Watch).

Common questions

Logalyst can't read or save some of my data

Logalyst only works with the Health data types you allow. Open the Health app, tap your profile picture, then Apps › Logalyst, and turn on the types you want to log. On Apple Watch, approve the permission sheet the first time you open the app there.

My widgets or complications show old values

Health can't be read while your iPhone is locked, so widgets show the last values they read until you unlock. Daily totals reset at midnight.

How do I edit or delete an entry?

In the History tab, swipe to delete an entry, or tap it to fix it. Foods can be fixed there or in the Nutrition tab: tap one to change its servings, weight or volume when recorded, meal, or time. You can also delete entries in the Health app.

History says an edit didn't finish

Health can't change an entry, so Logalyst saves the corrected one and then deletes the original. If the original couldn't be deleted (for example because your iPhone locked), both are listed until you tap Remove Original, or Keep Both to leave them and delete one yourself. If Logalyst closed partway through, it finishes the edit the next time it opens.

Why can't I log a food by weight?

A food needs its serving weight in grams. Swipe left on it in Add Food, tap Edit, and fill in Serving Weight (or tap the button that uses the weight its serving size states). Foods measured only by volume do not have a known weight; you can log them by volume when their serving volume is known.

How do I log a liquid food by volume?

Edit the food and fill in Serving Volume with the amount its nutrition describes. For nutrition per 100 mL, enter 100 mL; logging 180 mL then scales each known nutrient by 1.8. You can use mL, U.S. fluid ounces or Imperial fluid ounces, and switching units keeps the measured amount. U.S. and Imperial fluid ounces differ from each other and from weight ounces. Use the system stated on the label; an ambiguous “fl oz” or a photo alone cannot establish a volume conversion.

Barcode, label and published-nutrition imports fill in a compatible volume when it is stated clearly. Otherwise, keep logging by servings until you can confirm it. A recipe's ingredients can have volumes, but the finished recipe is logged by servings. Replacing a measured food with one that lacks a compatible basis asks you to confirm a serving amount.

Why does nutrition say unavailable or partial?

A blank field means the nutrient is unknown. Enter 0 only when zero is stated; it stays different from a blank field. Meals and recipes add the values that are known and show how many included ingredients are missing each nutrient. An entirely unknown nutrient is unavailable. A known subtotal with missing ingredients is partial. Foods you leave out do not increase the missing count.

A food needs at least one known nutrient, including a stated zero, to log directly. If a meal contains a food with no known nutrition, add a known value or leave that food out before logging. When another ingredient has nutrition, you can instead use Save as Recipe to keep the unknown ingredient and its missing-nutrition indicators with the rest of the meal. Unknown values are never filled in as zero.

Daily totals include intake other apps record in Health. Older entries or other apps may not provide enough information to establish complete coverage. No recorded data does not prove nothing was consumed, and Health does not reveal whether an empty result means read access was denied. Widgets and spoken totals also qualify missing or partial data. Estimate and Published still describe where the figures came from; neither means that every nutrient is known.

How do I use a restaurant's nutrition for a photo of my meal?

After you take or choose the photo in Photo of Meal, type the restaurant or brand, such as Chipotle, and add any details the photo can't show, like “double chicken, white rice”. Tap Look Up Nutrition. Logalyst finds the nutrition the brand publishes on its own website: foods marked Published use the brand's figures, with a link to where they came from, and the rest are Apple Intelligence's estimates. If a food could be one of several, like white or brown rice, tap Choose; if it might be hidden under other foods, tap Include if it was there. Only the brand's name is sent (to Apple Maps, to find its website), and your photo stays on your iPhone. Leave the brand blank to keep everything on your iPhone.

The restaurant lookup didn't find anything

Some restaurants publish their nutrition only inside an interactive calculator, or in a layout Logalyst can't read reliably. You'll see Apple Intelligence's estimates instead, with a link to the brand's nutrition page: check the figures there, then swipe left on a food and replace it with one of yours. If you were offline, tap Try Again.

My Watch doesn't show a preset I added on my iPhone

Presets are kept per unit, and the Watch shows the ones for the unit it enters in, which follows your Health app units (or your region), not the units you pick in Logalyst's Options. Open the Watch app once your iPhone is nearby to receive changes.

My saved foods didn't show up on my new iPhone

Saved foods, recipes and settings sync through your iCloud account. Make sure you're signed in to iCloud and that Logalyst is turned on under Settings › [your name] › iCloud.

Some food details changed after using an older app version

Older versions can drop serving volumes or nutrition-coverage details when they rewrite foods and recipes, and may discard explicit zeros. Update both devices and review the serving basis and missing nutrients after editing on an older version. Logalyst cannot reconstruct information that the older version removed.

Siri doesn't understand my request

Every Siri phrase has to include the app's name, for example “Log water in Logalyst” or “Log my weight in Logalyst”.

Where is my data stored?

Your health data stays in Apple Health on your devices. See the privacy policy for details.
